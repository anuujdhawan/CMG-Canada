### Intro (target: "LMIA consultant Brampton", "LMIA application Ontario")
A Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) is the document that lets a Canadian employer hire a foreign worker where no Canadian citizen or permanent resident is available to fill the role. Our Brampton office has filed over 500 LMIAs across every major stream for employers in Peel Region and the GTA — warehousing, manufacturing, healthcare, trucking, agriculture and skilled trades — and we build every file to withstand an ESDC compliance review, not just to get an initial approval.

### H2: LMIA streams we handle
- **High-wage LMIA** — for roles paid above the provincial median hourly wage, with a transition plan requirement demonstrating a path to reduced reliance on temporary foreign workers.
- **Low-wage LMIA** — for roles below the median wage, subject to caps on the proportion of low-wage workers at a single worksite and additional employer obligations (return transportation, private health insurance until provincial coverage begins, and employer-paid recruitment costs).
- **Agricultural LMIA** — seasonal and year-round streams for the agriculture and agri-food sector, including the Seasonal Agricultural Worker Program and the Agricultural Stream.
- **Global Talent Stream** — expedited processing for eligible tech and in-demand occupations; see our [dedicated Global Talent Stream page](/for-employers/global-talent-stream).

### H2: The recruitment step ESDC actually checks
Before an LMIA is approved, ESDC reviews whether the employer made genuine efforts to hire domestically — advertising duration, platforms used, and how applicants were assessed. This is the step most self-filed LMIAs get wrong, either by under-advertising or by documenting recruitment inconsistently with the job description on file. We build the recruitment record alongside the LMIA application itself, not after ESDC asks for it.

### H2: Why Brampton & Peel Region employers work with us
Peel Region's warehousing, logistics and manufacturing sector relies heavily on the temporary foreign worker program, and our office sees the occupation-specific wage and advertising requirements ESDC applies to these roles daily. Being based in Brampton means we track regional wage data and processing patterns for the occupations that matter most to local employers, rather than treating every file as generic.

### H2: Our LMIA process
1. **Stream and wage classification** — high-wage vs. low-wage vs. specialized stream determination.
2. **Recruitment strategy** — advertising plan built to ESDC's minimum requirements for your stream.
3. **Application preparation** — transition plan (high-wage) or cap and obligation review (low-wage).
4. **Filing and ESDC liaison** — submission, responses to requests for information, and decision tracking.
5. **Post-approval support** — work permit application guidance for the named foreign worker.

### FAQ
**How long does an LMIA take to process in 2026?**
Processing times vary by stream and occupation — high-wage and low-wage LMIAs typically take several weeks, while stream-specific programs like the Global Talent Stream are expedited. We confirm current ESDC service standards for your occupation before you post the job.

**What's the difference between high-wage and low-wage LMIA streams?**
The classification depends on whether the offered wage is above or below the median hourly wage for that occupation in Ontario. Each stream carries different advertising requirements, transition plan obligations, and worksite caps.

**Do I need to advertise the position before applying?**
Yes, in most streams — a minimum advertising period across specified platforms with genuine recruitment efforts documented, with limited exemptions for certain occupations and streams.
