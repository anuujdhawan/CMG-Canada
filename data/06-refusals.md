### H1
Visa Refusal & Procedural Fairness Response — Brampton-Based RCICs, Filing Across Canada

### Intro (target: "visa refusal consultant Brampton", "procedural fairness letter response")
A refusal is a decision made on the file an officer actually saw — not necessarily the strongest version of your case. Our Brampton team specializes in taking refused files, isolating what the officer's notes actually say, and building the next submission around that specific gap rather than resubmitting a generic reapplication that repeats the same mistake.

### H2: Refusal types we handle
- **Visitor visa (TRV) refusals** — insufficient ties, unclear purpose of visit, or unexplained funds.
- **Study permit refusals** — weak study plan, insufficient proof of funds, or DLI/program concerns.
- **Work permit refusals** — LMIA or exemption issues, unclear employer-employee relationship, or NOC mismatch.
- **Spousal & family sponsorship refusals** — genuineness concerns or eligibility gaps.
- **Express Entry / PR refusals** — misrepresentation concerns, medical inadmissibility, or missing documentation at the ITA stage.
- **PGWP & restoration refusals** — program eligibility disputes or status-gap issues.

### H2: Procedural Fairness Letters (PFL) — the 15–30 day window
A PFL means IRCC has a specific concern about your file and is giving you a defined window — typically 15 to 30 days — to respond before a final decision. This is not a formality. A well-structured PFL response directly answers the stated concern with evidence, rather than restating the original application. We treat every PFL as a hard deadline from the day it arrives.

### H2: GCMS notes — what actually happened in your file
The refusal letter you receive is often a short, templated summary. The GCMS (Global Case Management System) notes behind it — obtained via an Access to Information and Privacy (ATIP) request — usually show the officer's actual reasoning line by line. For any refusal where the stated reason feels incomplete, requesting and reading the GCMS notes is the first step before deciding on reapplication or appeal.

### H2: Reapplication, IAD appeal, or judicial review?
- **Reapplication** — appropriate when the refusal reasons can be directly addressed with stronger evidence or corrected documentation.
- **Immigration Appeal Division (IAD)** — the formal appeal route for refused spousal, partner and some family class sponsorships, generally within 30 days of the refusal.
- **Judicial review (Federal Court)** — for procedural fairness breaches or unreasonable decisions where no other appeal route exists; requires leave (permission) from the Court and has strict filing deadlines.

### FAQ
**How long do I have to respond to a PFL?**
Typically 15 to 30 days, as stated in the letter. Missing the deadline generally means the refusal is finalized as-is.

**What are GCMS notes and why do they matter?**
They're the officer's internal case notes, obtained via an ATIP request, and often reveal the actual reasoning behind a refusal in more detail than the refusal letter.

**Should I reapply or appeal?**
Depends on the category. Spousal sponsorship refusals have a formal IAD appeal route; other refusals are often better addressed with a stronger, evidence-backed reapplication, or in limited cases, Federal Court judicial review.
