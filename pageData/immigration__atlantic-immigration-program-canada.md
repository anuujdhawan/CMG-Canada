# Atlantic Immigration Program Canada: a practical PR plan

The Atlantic Immigration Program (AIP) is an employer-driven pathway to permanent residence for eligible skilled foreign workers and international graduates who have a qualifying job offer from a designated employer in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia or Prince Edward Island. This guide is a starting point; IRCC’s live instructions control current eligibility, forms, fees and processing.

- candidates with an Atlantic job offer or a potential designated employer
- international graduates from a recognized post-secondary institution in Atlantic Canada
- employers and families planning a compliant move to Atlantic Canada

## Page Content

## What is the Atlantic Immigration Program?

The Atlantic Immigration Program is a federal permanent-residence pathway designed to help designated employers in Atlantic Canada hire qualified international workers and graduates. It is not a points draw and it is not a provincial nominee program. The route depends on a qualifying job offer, a provincial endorsement, settlement planning and a complete permanent-residence application to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC).

The four participating provinces are New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia and Prince Edward Island. You can be living outside Canada or in Canada as a temporary resident, but you must genuinely plan to work and settle in the Atlantic province connected to your job offer.

The program can be a strong fit when your employer, occupation, language results, education, work history and settlement plan tell the same story. It is not a shortcut around admissibility, document requirements or the need to maintain valid temporary status when you are already in Canada.

## Who can apply for the Atlantic Immigration Program?

You generally need all of the following before applying for permanent residence under AIP:

- a valid full-time, non-seasonal job offer from a designated employer in Atlantic Canada
- qualifying paid work experience, unless you qualify for the international-graduate exemption
- the required level of education for the job offer
- an approved language test in English or French
- enough settlement funds, unless you are already working in Canada with a valid work permit
- a settlement plan prepared with an approved service provider organization
- a provincial endorsement connected to the job offer
- admissibility to Canada and a complete application package

An AIP application is evidence-led. The job offer, NOC duties, work reference letters, language results, education, family information and settlement plan should be consistent before you file.

## AIP eligibility requirements to check

### Work experience

Most candidates need at least 1,560 hours of related paid work experience during the five years before applying. That is broadly equivalent to 30 hours per week for one year, and the hours must have been accumulated over at least one year. Volunteer work, unpaid internships and self-employed work do not meet this requirement. Canadian work must have been authorized.

Your experience must match the main duties of the applicable NOC and meet the relationship between the TEER level of your work history and the TEER level of the job offer. For example, a TEER 3 job offer generally requires qualifying experience in TEER 3 or 4. Confirm the current NOC description and the exact AIP table before relying on a work history.

Recent international graduates may be exempt from the work-experience requirement if they completed an eligible credential of at least two years at a recognized post-secondary institution in Atlantic Canada, graduated within the required period, studied full time, lived in an Atlantic province for at least 16 months during the two years before graduating, and held the required authorization while in Canada. The exemption has specific exclusions, including certain programs where distance learning or second-language study makes up a substantial part of the program.

### Language

You must take an approved language test and meet the minimum in all four abilities. The minimum is generally **CLB 5** for TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 job offers and **CLB 4** for a TEER 4 job offer. IRCC accepts approved tests such as CELPIP-General, IELTS General Training, PTE Core, TEF Canada and TCF Canada. Results must normally be less than two years old when you apply.

### Education

For a TEER 0 or 1 offer, you generally need a Canadian one-year post-secondary credential or higher, or the foreign equivalent. For a TEER 2, 3 or 4 offer, you generally need a Canadian high-school diploma or higher, or the foreign equivalent. A foreign credential usually requires an Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) for immigration, and regulated occupations may require separate licensing or certification before you can work.

### Settlement funds

If you are not already working in Canada with a valid work permit, you must show that you have accessible funds to support yourself and your family after arrival. Include your spouse or common-law partner and dependent children when determining family size, even if they are not accompanying you. IRCC’s current proof-of-funds page controls the amount; do not rely on an old calculator or screenshot.

### Job offer and genuine intent

The offer must be full-time, at least 30 hours per week and non-seasonal. For TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3, it must generally last at least one year from the date you become a permanent resident. A TEER 4 offer must generally be permanent with no end date. The employer must be designated by the relevant Atlantic province, and you must be able and willing to perform the work. A job offer from a company majority-owned by you, your spouse or common-law partner is not eligible.

## Finding a valid AIP job offer

You cannot create an AIP application from a generic job offer. Start by confirming that the organization is designated in the province where you will work and that the position meets the program’s offer requirements. Your employer should provide confirmation of designation and the completed **Offer of Employment to a Foreign National under the Atlantic Immigration Program (IMM 0157)** for your records.

The employer and candidate then work with a settlement service provider organization to prepare a settlement plan. The employer submits the endorsement application to the province. If the province endorses the application, you receive a Certificate of Endorsement, which is needed for the permanent-residence application.

An endorsement is not the same as permanent residence. IRCC still assesses the complete application, including admissibility, forms, supporting documents, biometrics, medical examination requirements and police certificates.

## How the Atlantic Immigration Program application works

1. Confirm that your job offer, work history, language, education, funds and family facts fit the current IRCC requirements.
2. Obtain a qualifying offer from an AIP-designated employer in one of the four Atlantic provinces.
3. Work with a settlement service provider organization and send the settlement plan to your employer.
4. Allow the employer to submit the provincial endorsement application.
5. Receive the Certificate of Endorsement and check every detail against your intended PR application.
6. Apply online through the PR Portal with the current document checklist, forms, fees and supporting evidence.
7. Respond to IRCC requests, complete biometrics or medical steps when required, and keep your contact and family information current.
8. Maintain valid temporary status if you are in Canada while your application is being processed.

IRCC states that an incomplete application can be rejected and must be corrected and resubmitted. A document checklist is not a substitute for checking whether the evidence actually proves your specific work, education, family and status history.

## Can you get a work permit while your PR application is processed?

Some AIP candidates who are eligible to apply for permanent residence may also qualify for an optional, employer-specific temporary work permit while the PR application is processed. The AIP work permit is generally valid for two years and only authorizes work for the employer who offered the qualifying job. It does not automatically lead to PR approval.

The work-permit application may require the provincial referral letter, an offer-of-employment number, the signed IMM 0156 undertaking and evidence of language, education and work experience. Your spouse or common-law partner may be eligible to apply for an open work permit, subject to the current IRCC rules. Check the live work-permit instructions before applying, especially if your current Canadian status is close to expiry.

## Documents to prepare for an AIP application

Build the file around a dated timeline and keep names, addresses and employment dates consistent. Depending on your situation, prepare:

- passport and identity documents for you and accompanying family members
- the AIP job offer, employer designation confirmation and IMM 0157
- the Certificate of Endorsement and settlement plan
- approved language-test results
- diplomas, transcripts and an ECA where required
- detailed employer reference letters, contracts, pay records and tax evidence for qualifying work
- proof of funds, unless the current exemption applies
- police certificates, medical information and photographs as requested
- proof of temporary status and authorized work or study in Canada, if applying from inside Canada
- certified translations for documents that are not in English or French

## Questions people ask

### Is the Atlantic Immigration Program open?

IRCC currently lists the Atlantic Immigration Program as open, but status, eligibility requirements, forms, fees, settlement-fund amounts and processing information can change. Check the live [IRCC AIP page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/atlantic-immigration/how-to-immigrate.html) immediately before preparing or submitting an application.

### Do I need an Express Entry profile for AIP?

No. AIP is a separate employer-driven permanent-residence pathway and is not an Express Entry draw. You still need a qualifying job offer, provincial endorsement and a complete application that meets IRCC’s current requirements.

### Does an AIP job offer guarantee permanent residence?

No. A job offer is only one part of the process. The employer must be designated, the offer must qualify, the province must endorse the candidate and IRCC must approve the complete application after assessing eligibility and admissibility.

### Can my spouse work in Canada while I apply through AIP?

Your spouse or common-law partner may be eligible for an open work permit in some circumstances. Eligibility depends on the current IRCC instructions and your application facts, so verify the requirements before applying.
