## What is a TRP?

Think of a Temporary Resident Permit (TRP) as a carefully considered exception rather than a cure. It is a document that allows someone who is inadmissible to Canada to enter or remain in the country for a limited period - but only when there are compelling reasons to justify it. Crucially, a TRP does not erase inadmissibility; it simply grants temporary access while the underlying situation remains. TRPs can respond to criminal, medical, or other grounds of inadmissibility.

## When TRP Is Appropriate vs Criminal Rehabilitation

The right tool depends entirely on your goal. Choose a TRP when you need to enter or stay in Canada temporarily and you are not yet eligible for Criminal Rehabilitation - or when you only need a one-time or short-term visit. Choose Criminal Rehabilitation when you want a permanent solution and you have passed the required waiting period after completing your sentence. The two are not mutually exclusive: you can apply for a TRP while your rehabilitation application is in process, which is a strategy we regularly use. For the legal framework, [IRCC's inadmissibility pages](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/inadmissibility.html) are a useful reference.

## Grounds Covered

TRPs can be used for criminal inadmissibility, medical inadmissibility (where permitted by policy), financial reasons in some cases, and other grounds such as security or misrepresentation concerns. Whatever the ground, the officer must be satisfied that your need to enter or stay in Canada outweighs the risk to Canada. That balancing exercise is where a well-prepared submission makes all the difference.

## Single-Entry vs Multiple-Entry TRP

A single-entry TRP permits one entry into Canada for its validity period. A multiple-entry TRP lets you leave and re-enter during its validity, which suits clients who need to travel back and forth - for business, family, or ongoing medical care. The number of entries and the length of validity depend on your reason for travel and the officer's assessment of your case.

## TRP Validity and Renewal

TRPs are issued for a set period - sometimes days, sometimes up to three years. When a TRP expires, you must either leave Canada or apply for a new one if you still have a continuing compelling reason. There is no automatic renewal; every application is assessed on its own merits, which is why keeping a documented, up-to-date justification matters.

## Application Process (POE vs IRCC)

You have two routes. You can apply for a TRP at a port of entry (POE) when you arrive at the border, or in advance through IRCC (at a visa office or online). Applying in advance removes much of the uncertainty of a border decision and lets you prepare your documentation calmly. Urgent or one-time travel may still be handled at the POE. We help you choose the approach that fits your situation and prepare the application properly.

## Compelling Reasons - What IRCC Considers

Officers weigh several factors: the reason for your visit (a wedding, a business deal, urgent medical treatment), the seriousness of your inadmissibility, the time that has passed since the offence or incident, and your ties to both Canada and your home country. A strong submission tells a coherent story - why you must be in Canada, why the risk is limited, and why you will leave when the permit expires.

## Cost and Processing Time

The TRP processing fee is $229 CAD. Processing times vary by office, so it is worth checking [IRCC's current processing times](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/check-processing-times.html). Urgent requests may be considered in some cases, but do not plan around that - apply as early as you can.

## TRP to PR Pathway

In rare cases, after an extended period in Canada on TRPs - for example, 3 years under certain public policies - an applicant may become eligible to apply for permanent residence. This is the exception, not the norm, and every situation is fact-specific. We can advise honestly on whether any such pathway could apply to you.
