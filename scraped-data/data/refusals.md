## What is a Procedural Fairness Letter?

A Procedural Fairness Letter (PFL) is IRCC's way of saying: "we are about to refuse, unless you change our mind." It lays out the officer's concerns and gives you one chance to respond before a final decision is made.

A PFL is not yet a refusal - but ignoring it, or replying with an emotional and unstructured letter, almost always leads to refusal and can even trigger misrepresentation findings. A structured, evidence-based response can genuinely reverse the outcome.

- • Officer must explain their concerns and give you a chance to respond.
- • Your response becomes part of the permanent GCMS record on your file.
- • Poorly handled PFLs can make future applications much harder to win.
Our refusal team requests your GCMS notes, maps every concern to the law and program criteria, and builds a targeted response with documents and legal argument - filed before your deadline.

## GCMS Notes - Your Secret Weapon

GCMS (Global Case Management System) notes are the immigration officer's internal record of your file - including their reasoning, concerns, and assessment.

The refusal letter is often vague; GCMS notes are not. They reveal the specific issues that led to your refusal, which is why we request them for almost every refusal case. Without GCMS notes, you are guessing. With them, you are targeting.

- ✓ Reveal officer's exact concerns
- ✓ Available through ATIP request
- ✓ Critical for targeted PFL responses
Did you know?

GCMS notes are available to applicants under Canada's Access to Information Act. We request these on your behalf as part of our refusal response service.
