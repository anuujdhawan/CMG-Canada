## What is Criminal Inadmissibility?

If you have been convicted of a crime in Canada or abroad, you may be criminally inadmissible to Canada. Here is the crucial detail: Canadian law does not simply take the foreign charge at face value. It looks at whether the offence has a Canadian equivalent and how serious that equivalent would be. Serious crimes - and, since 2018, many DUIs - can lead to long-term or even permanent inadmissibility unless you overcome it through rehabilitation or a TRP.

## How Canadian Law Classifies Foreign Offences

IRCC and CBSA assess two questions: would the foreign offence also be an offence in Canada, and is it "serious" or "ordinary" criminality? That classification drives everything - whether you need Criminal Rehabilitation (plus its waiting period) or whether deemed rehabilitation might apply. Legal opinions and court documents are often used to support your case, and getting the equivalency right is where an experienced RCIC earns their keep. IRCC's [admissibility guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/inadmissibility.html) explains the general framework.

## Deemed Rehabilitation

In some situations, enough time has passed since you completed your sentence that Canada considers you rehabilitated automatically - no application required. Whether this applies depends on the type of offence and the number of years elapsed. Not every offence qualifies, so it is always worth having your situation assessed rather than assuming you are eligible (or ineligible).

## Criminal Rehabilitation Application

A successful Criminal Rehabilitation application is the permanent fix for that specific offence - once granted, you generally never need to address it again. You must demonstrate that you have been rehabilitated and that the required time has passed since completing your sentence. The application goes to IRCC with supporting documents and a processing fee.

## Temporary Resident Permit (TRP)

A TRP lets you enter or remain in Canada temporarily despite inadmissibility when there are compelling reasons. It can be single-entry or multiple-entry. It does not erase inadmissibility - it only grants temporary access, which makes it the ideal bridge while you wait to become eligible for rehabilitation.

## DUI / Impaired Driving

DUI and impaired driving are now treated as serious criminality in Canada in many cases, which has pulled a great many people into inadmissibility who previously would not have been affected. Your options are the same toolkit: a TRP for temporary entry, or Criminal Rehabilitation after the required waiting period (often 5 years after sentence completion). Processing times and eligibility vary with the specifics of your case. We handle both TRP and rehabilitation applications for DUI and other offences.

## Individual vs Deemed Rehabilitation

Deemed rehabilitation happens automatically once the required time has passed - but only for eligible offences. Individual (formal) rehabilitation requires an actual application and IRCC approval. If you do not qualify for deemed rehabilitation, individual rehabilitation is the route once the waiting period has passed.

## Record Suspension (Pardon) and Canadian Inadmissibility

A Canadian record suspension (pardon) can, in some cases, reduce or remove the effect of a Canadian conviction for immigration purposes. For foreign convictions, the country where the offence occurred may have its own pardon or equivalent, and Canada may take that into account. We can advise on how a pardon or record suspension affects your admissibility.

## Legal Opinion Letters

In complex cases, a legal opinion letter from a Canadian immigration or criminal lawyer can help explain a foreign offence and its Canadian equivalent. We work with clients to assemble the right evidence and, where appropriate, refer you to legal counsel for formal opinions.
