## New Brunswick's Community Interest Letter Is the Most-Scrutinized Document in Atlantic PNP

Officers in New Brunswick have reviewed thousands of Community Interest Letters, and they reject generic essays on sight. A letter that says "New Brunswick is a wonderful province with a high quality of life" provides no usable evidence of intent. NBPNP requires documented, verifiable proof that you have genuinely researched and committed to building a life in New Brunswick — not that you are willing to live there in exchange for permanent residence.

VMC builds evidence packages for every NB client: employment history in NB, family connection diagrams with supporting documents, housing search records with dates, school research for children, documented exploratory visits, and community tie commitments. These packages are assembled around the officer's actual evaluation framework — never a template. The difference between an approved letter and a rejected one is almost always the quality of the supporting evidence, not the eloquence of the writing.

## Population Growth Through Evidence-Based Intent

New Brunswick is Atlantic Canada's fastest-growing province by recent immigration intake — and the provincial nominee program reflects that ambition. NBPNP demands more documented proof of settlement intent than most other provinces.

### Fastest-Growing Atlantic Province

New Brunswick's population reached approximately 830,000 in 2025, with immigration accounting for virtually all of the province's recent population growth. NB has benefited from significant federal and provincial immigration investment, with Moncton and Fredericton emerging as technology and innovation corridors that attract skilled immigrants from across Canada and internationally. The province's aggressive immigration targets reflect both economic necessity and a deliberate strategy to offset demographic aging in rural communities. This context matters for applicants: NB wants immigrants who will stay and contribute — which is why settlement intent scrutiny here is higher than in provinces with more diversified labour markets.

### Bilingual Province - Significant French Advantage

New Brunswick is Canada's only officially bilingual province. Approximately 32% of the provincial population is francophone, with the highest concentrations in the Moncton region, Bathurst, and the Acadian Peninsula on the northeast coast. French-speaking immigration candidates benefit from access to Francophone-targeted NBPNP streams and draws, higher EOI scores where bilingual capacity is a factor, and the +600 CRS federal bonus available to French-speaking candidates in federal Express Entry draws. Candidates with tested French proficiency at CLB 7 or higher should ensure their bilingual status is fully documented and leveraged in every applicable pathway.

### Key NB Industries and Labour Priorities

New Brunswick's economy is anchored by five major sectors: technology and digital services (concentrated in Fredericton's Killarney Lake tech district and Moncton's bilingual call centre and software ecosystem), healthcare and social assistance (with acute nursing shortages in rural hospitals and long-term care facilities), agri-food processing (potatoes, blueberries, seafood — particularly in rural communities), manufacturing (aerospace parts, forestry products, modular construction), and construction and trades (driven by housing demand from immigration itself). NBPNP draws have consistently prioritized healthcare and technology occupations alongside construction trades.

### NBPNP Scrutinizes Settlement Intent More Than Most Provinces

New Brunswick has an above-average rate of immigration retention challenges — immigrants who obtain nomination through NBPNP but subsequently move to larger cities in Ontario or Quebec after receiving permanent residence. In response, NBPNP has intensified its scrutiny of the Community Interest Letter and the settlement intent evidence it requires. Officers are trained to distinguish genuine provincial connection from transactional applications. This is not bureaucratic caution — it reflects real provincial interest in retaining the immigrants it nominates. Applicants who can document genuine reasons to build a life in NB — employer ties, family, prior residency, bilingual community access — succeed. Those who cannot provide evidence beyond wishful statements struggle.

## The Document That Makes or Breaks Your NBPNP Application

No other document in the NBPNP package receives as much officer attention as the Community Interest Letter. This section explains what officers look for, what gets rejected, and how VMC builds the evidence matrix that supports approval.

### What Officers Look For

NBPNP officers evaluate Community Interest Letters against a mental checklist that mirrors the province's stated goals for immigration: economic integration and long-term retention. They look for:

- Specific community research: Named communities, neighbourhoods, or municipalities with researched details — not "New Brunswick cities" in general.
- Employer support letters: Signed by the employer with business registration, confirming position title, wage, hours, and indeterminate status. Not a generic reference letter.
- Family connection diagrams: Named relatives with their status (PR or citizen), NB address documentation, and your relationship explained with supporting records.
- Housing search evidence: Screenshots of NB real estate or rental listings with dates visible, showing you have actively researched where you would live.
- School research for children: Named specific NB schools, their contact information, and any enrollment inquiry confirmation if your children will attend.
- Volunteer or community tie commitments: Named organizations in NB where you intend to volunteer, with any correspondence showing you have reached out.
- Exploratory visit documentation: Travel records, accommodation receipts, and notes from employer or community visits showing you have physically been to NB.

### What Gets Rejected

Based on VMC's experience with NB officer feedback and refusal reasons, these are the letter types that consistently fail:

- Generic quality-of-life essays: "New Brunswick has excellent healthcare, a safe environment, and a welcoming community" — this describes every province and proves nothing about NB-specific intent.
- Unverified family claims: Mentioning relatives in NB without attaching their PR card, citizenship certificate, or proof of NB address.
- Housing plans without evidence: "I plan to rent an apartment in Moncton" without any attached housing search records.
- Future tense without action: All statements about what you "will do" after arriving with no evidence of steps already taken.
- Employer letters not on letterhead: A plain text email from the employer does not carry the same weight as a formal letter on company letterhead with contact information and signature.
- Template language matching known formats: Officers see patterns in letter language. Template-based letters are identifiable and trigger additional scrutiny of the rest of the package.

## Five NBPNP Streams - Choosing the Right One

NBPNP offers multiple entry points depending on your occupation, language profile, employer status, and NB connection. Applying under the wrong stream is a common cause of wasted time.

### Express Entry New Brunswick

Linked to the federal Express Entry pool. NB draws from the federal pool targeting candidates with documented New Brunswick ties — prior work or study in NB, family in NB, or occupations aligned with NB priority sectors. Candidates must have an active federal EE profile with CRS typically 300+ and must submit an Expression of Interest to NBPNP separately from their federal profile.

Upon nomination, the +600 CRS ensures a federal ITA in the next appropriate draw. Processing time from NB nomination to federal PR is typically 6-12 months via Express Entry. NB connection documentation is required even for this stream — a federal EE profile alone is not sufficient.

- Active federal EE profile required
- NB connection or priority NOC required
- Community Interest Letter still assessed
- +600 CRS upon nomination

### Skilled Worker with Employer Support

The primary employer-supported stream for skilled occupations. Requires an indeterminate, full-time job offer from a New Brunswick employer in a TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 occupation. The employer must demonstrate active local recruitment before supporting a foreign national. The worker may be in NB on a valid work permit or abroad with a confirmed offer.

- TEER 0-3 occupations
- Indeterminate full-time NB job offer
- Employer local recruitment documentation
- Language: generally CLB 5+ (varies by NOC TEER)
- Community Interest Letter required
- Minimum one year qualifying work experience

### Critical Worker

Targets intermediate occupations — including some TEER 4 roles — where New Brunswick has documented persistent labour shortages. Language floors are lower than the Skilled Worker stream, reflecting occupational requirements. An NB employer is required. Wage must meet or exceed the provincial median for the specific occupation.

Critical Worker draws are less frequent and more targeted. Seafood processing, food services, commercial transportation, agricultural work, and some healthcare support roles have historically qualified. The Community Interest Letter remains a requirement.

- TEER 4 eligible in shortage occupations
- NB employer with documented shortage
- Language floor: CLB 4 typically
- Wage at or above NB median for NOC

### NB Strategic Initiative / Pilot Programs

NB periodically opens targeted streams or pilots for specific occupational shortages, community needs, or sector priorities. French-speaking candidates are frequently favored in these pilots. Active pilots in 2025-2026 have included targeted technology occupations in Fredericton and Moncton, rural community healthcare workers, and Francophone Atlantic initiatives.

These streams open and close without long advance notice. VMC monitors active NBPNP pilots and can confirm whether a current stream matches your profile before you begin preparing documentation.

- Criteria vary by pilot - confirm active streams
- French language often advantaged
- Community connection still required
- Can open and close with short notice

### Entrepreneur Stream

For entrepreneurs who will establish, purchase, or invest in an active New Brunswick business. Requires minimum personal net worth of approximately $500,000 CAD (verified by a licensed accountant) and a minimum investment of approximately $125,000 in a qualifying NB business. A mandatory exploratory visit to NB and a business plan review with NBPNP are required before application.

Provisional nominees must sign a performance agreement with NB confirming business establishment and job creation milestones. A performance bond is held until milestones are confirmed. Final nomination is issued after the province verifies the business is operating as planned.

- Net worth: minimum ~$500K CAD (verified)
- Investment: minimum ~$125K in NB business
- Exploratory visit mandatory
- Business plan + performance agreement
- NB employment creation required

Confirm your stream before preparing any documents

VMC reviews your NOC, employer NB registration, language scores, and provincial connection in a free consultation before recommending a stream. The wrong stream means the wrong Community Interest Letter strategy.

## Bilingual Candidates Have a Significant Edge in New Brunswick

New Brunswick is Canada's only officially bilingual province. French language proficiency opens additional NBPNP streams, improves EOI scores, and unlocks a federal Express Entry bonus.

### French Language Opens Four Doors at Once

- NBPNP Francophone Streams and Priority Draws: NB periodically runs draws that specifically target French-speaking candidates. Bilingual applicants may be invited in draws that English-only candidates of higher overall score cannot access.
- Higher EOI Scores in General Streams: In NBPNP's general EOI scoring, French proficiency is an additional factor that increases ranking. A bilingual candidate at CLB 5 English and CLB 7 French scores higher than an English-only candidate at CLB 7 English with otherwise equivalent profiles.
- Federal Express Entry +600 CRS Bonus: French-speaking candidates who receive NBPNP nominations and are in the federal EE pool benefit from the standard +600 CRS nomination. Additionally, candidates with strong French proficiency may receive higher base CRS scores from IRCC's Francophone draws.
- Moncton as a Bilingual Tech and Business Hub: Moncton's bilingual workforce is actively sought by technology companies, financial services firms, and call centre operations. French-speaking candidates targeting Moncton employers are entering one of Atlantic Canada's most dynamic labour markets for bilingual professionals.

### French Language Documentation Requirements

To claim French language advantage in NBPNP and federal pathways, proficiency must be tested by an approved organization. Accepted French language tests include TEF Canada (Test d'évaluation de français) and TCF Canada (Test de connaissance du français). Results must be less than 2 years old from the date of the test.

CLB 7 is the typical threshold for accessing Francophone-specific streams and draws. CLB 5 French with CLB 5+ English opens additional points in general streams. If you speak French but have never taken a formal language test, taking TEF or TCF should be a priority step in your NBPNP preparation.

NB Francophone Communities

The highest concentrations of French-speaking communities in NB include Moncton / Dieppe / Riverview (largest bilingual metro in Atlantic Canada), Bathurst and the Acadian Peninsula, Edmundston in the northwest, Caraquet, and rural Acadian communities throughout northeastern and eastern NB.

## AIP - Federal-Provincial PR for All Four Atlantic Provinces

AIP complements New Brunswick's own nominee streams. It is employer-driven: your CRS ranking is irrelevant if you meet program criteria and a designated employer endorses you. Full program details are on [IRCC's Atlantic Immigration Program page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/atlantic-immigration.html).

Key AIP facts (verify IRCC + provincial manuals)

- Designated employer must obtain provincial endorsement before you file PR.
- Full-time permanent job offer from that employer at required skill level.
- Skilled worker path: TEER 0-3 occupations typically need ≥1 year qualifying experience (or eligible exemptions).
- International graduate path: credential from publicly funded Atlantic institution with defined study duration.
- Language: commonly CLB 4-5+ depending on NOC TEER - confirm current tables.
- Settlement plan with designated service provider organization is mandatory.
- Government processing fee currently $1,365 CAD to IRCC (2026 rate - verify fee schedule).
- Typical timeline: roughly 6-12 months from complete submission to PR decision, file-dependent.

VMC pairs employer designation paperwork with worker eligibility so endorsement and IRCC stages stay synchronized.

## How to Apply for NBPNP - From Stream Confirmation to PR

The Community Interest Letter is the most time-consuming element to build correctly. Begin that process before you confirm your stream — the evidence you gather determines which stream arguments are strongest.

### Confirm stream eligibility (job offer, NOC, language, NB connection)

Verify your primary NOC and its TEER level. Confirm your NB employer is registered in the province and qualifies as an eligible employer. Review your language test results against the floor for your stream. Identify every NB connection you can document — even minor connections (a prior visit, a distant relative, a previous job application to an NB employer) may be worth including.

### Draft Community Interest Letter with full evidence package

The letter and its supporting documents are prepared together — not sequentially. VMC maps each claim in the letter to a specific attached document: the employer claim to the job offer letter, the family claim to the relative's PR card and NB address proof, the housing claim to Realtor.ca screenshots with dates. Each section of the letter references specific attached evidence rather than making unsupported assertions.

### Gather employer authorization and compliance documentation

The employer package includes: NB business registration certificate, job offer letter on company letterhead with position title, wage, hours, and indeterminate status confirmed, NAICS code identification, evidence of local recruitment (job postings, interview documentation, written confirmation of unsuccessful searches), and the employer's declaration of their capacity to support the nominee. AIP cases additionally require employer designation application preparation.

### Submit NBPNP application or initiate AIP employer designation

File the complete NBPNP application through the provincial portal with all supporting documents. For AIP cases, initiate the employer designation application to the province simultaneously. Ensure all documents are in the required format and translation requirements are met for non-English, non-French documents.

### Respond to any officer requests promptly

NBPNP officers may request additional information or clarification on any aspect of the application. Responses to officer requests should be prompt (typically 30 days or less) and should provide exactly what was requested plus any additional context that reinforces the settlement intent evidence. Delayed or incomplete responses to officer inquiries are a common cause of application withdrawal.

### Submit federal PR via Express Entry +600 or base PNP channel

Express Entry-linked nominees: update your federal profile with the provincial nomination, receive the +600 CRS, accept the resulting federal ITA within 10 days, and submit a complete PR application within 60 days of the ITA. Base PNP nominees: file directly with IRCC through the paper-based or online PR application process. VMC coordinates both the provincial and federal stages to ensure no documentation gaps appear between the two steps.

## Common NBPNP Mistakes That Cause Refusals

These errors appear in cases that come to VMC after an initial refusal or officer request. Every one of them was preventable.

### Submitting a Generic Community Interest Letter Without Supporting Evidence

The single most common cause of NBPNP refusal. Officers are experienced at identifying letters that describe New Brunswick's attributes without proving the applicant's specific intent to establish there. A letter that is not attached to documented evidence — employer letters, housing searches, school research, travel records — is assessed as unverified intent. VMC builds the evidence package first and writes the letter around what can be proved.

### Not Confirming Employer's NAICS Code Compliance for AIP

AIP employer designation requires the employer's NAICS (industry) code to be in a permitted sector. Several NAICS codes are restricted or excluded from AIP designation — employers in these sectors cannot designate workers through AIP regardless of the job offer quality. Confirming NAICS code compliance before initiating an AIP employer designation saves weeks of preparation on an application that will be declined.

### Missing the French Language Advantage if Bilingual

Candidates with French proficiency at CLB 5+ who have not taken a formal French language test are leaving significant value on the table. NBPNP awards higher EOI scores to bilingual candidates. Access to Francophone-targeted draws can mean invitation when English-only streams are full. The federal +600 CRS nomination bonus works alongside Francophone Express Entry draws. A TEF or TCF test takes a few hours and can transform an application strategy.

### Applying Without a Verifiable NB Connection When NOI-Based

Some candidates apply to NBPNP based on a general Notice of Interest or Expression of Interest without a documented NB connection — hoping the job offer alone carries the Community Interest Letter. NB officers consistently assess the strength of the connection claim, not just the job offer. An application where the only NB tie is a very recent job offer without prior provincial contact tends to receive additional scrutiny.

### Failing to Update EOI After Material Changes

If your language scores improve, your employer changes, your NOC changes, or your NB connection strengthens (a family member gets PR, you visit NB, you get an upgraded job offer), your existing EOI must be updated. Stale EOIs that no longer reflect your actual profile are assessed against the outdated information — creating a mismatch between your claimed qualifications and your actual documents.

### Not Parallel-Tracking AIP When Your Employer Is Ready

NBPNP has its own nomination allocation which can fill earlier than expected in high-demand periods. Employer-ready candidates who wait for NBPNP to process while their employer qualifies for AIP designation are creating unnecessary single-path dependency. AIP bypasses NB's nomination quota entirely. VMC initiates AIP employer designation in parallel with NBPNP EOI preparation so clients have two active paths to PR simultaneously.
