## Manitoba's Provincial Nominee Program - The Basics

The Manitoba Provincial Nominee Program is the oldest provincial nominee program in Canada — launched in 1998, when Manitoba and Ottawa signed the first provincial immigration agreement in the country's history. Since then the MPNP has grown into a mature, regionally-driven program that nominates thousands of workers, graduates, and entrepreneurs every year. It is administered jointly by Manitoba's Ministry of Economic Development, Investment, Trade and Natural Resources and the Government of Canada: Manitoba nominates, and [IRCC](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship.html) makes the final decision on permanent residence.

The program runs three main streams: the Skilled Worker Stream (for people already in Manitoba, or overseas with a Manitoba connection), the International Education Stream (for graduates of Manitoba post-secondary institutions), and the Business Investor Stream (for entrepreneurs). All but the Business Investor Stream use an Expression of Interest (EOI) system — you build a profile, earn a points score, and wait to be selected through regular draws. Selected candidates receive a Letter of Advice to Apply (LAA) and then have 60 days to file a complete application.

What truly sets MPNP apart from every other PNP in Canada is its mandatory Manitoba connection requirement. You cannot even submit an EOI for the Skilled Worker Stream without demonstrating an established link to the province — through current Manitoba employment, previous Manitoba work or study, family or friend support in Manitoba, or a direct invitation from a strategic recruitment initiative. This is not a bonus you can skip; it is a hard eligibility gate. No connection, no application — regardless of your EOI score. In 2026 that gate has closed even tighter: every draw has been invitation-based, so Manitoba is selecting only candidates the province itself has already invited.

### Canada's First PNP - Over 25 Years of Provincial Immigration Leadership

The MPNP was created in 1998 under the Manitoba-Canada Agreement on Provincial Nominees — the first agreement of its kind in Canada. The program started with just 152 nominees in its inaugural year; today it nominates thousands annually. That longevity matters: Manitoba has spent more than 25 years refining its employer connections, community integration programs, and settlement infrastructure than most other provinces ever will. The MPNP is not an experiment — it is one of the most established, dependable routes to Canadian permanent residence in the country.

### Manitoba Connection Is Not Optional - It Is the Entry Requirement

Every skilled worker applicant must demonstrate a meaningful connection to Manitoba before an application can even be filed. Compare that with Alberta AAIP, where a qualifying job offer creates the connection, or Saskatchewan SINP, where overseas priority-sector workers mainly need a JAL — Manitoba ties the whole eligibility gateway to provincial attachment. So the strategic question is never "how do I score more points?" but always "how do I establish my Manitoba connection?" We help candidates build that connection through the Hire Global Talent employer division.

### Strategic Recruitment - Manitoba's Dominant Selection Model in 2026

Strategic recruitment initiatives are targeted programs run by Manitoba's government in partnership with employers, community organizations, Francophone groups, ethnocultural associations, and regional economic development bodies. When a Manitoba employer wants a specific worker, they file a request through MPNP's Employer Services initiative, and the MPNP issues a direct invitation. That invitation — combined with an active EOI profile declaring the invitation number — is what qualifies candidates for draws. In 2026, every draw has required exactly this. General EOIs without an invitation are simply not being selected.

### MPNP Nomination + Express Entry = ~6 Month Federal PR

The MPNP runs both base and enhanced pathways. The enhanced (Express Entry-linked) version adds 600 CRS points to your federal Express Entry profile when you receive the provincial nomination — in recent draws, comfortably above every published cutoff no matter your base CRS score. After nomination, federal permanent residence processing runs about 6 months. Each 2026 draw has included 10–20% of selected candidates holding valid Express Entry profiles, so the Manitoba-EE dual track is alive and well.

### Trend 1 - 100% Strategic Recruitment Selection

Every draw from #261 (January 2026) through #267 (March 2026) has exclusively targeted candidates declaring direct invitations from MPNP strategic recruitment initiatives. That is a sharp shift from even late 2025, when draws still occasionally pulled general-pool candidates by points ranking. Look at Draw #266 (March 12, 2026): 46 LAAs — 17 through Employer Services (37%), 12 through Francophone Community (26%), 9 through Ethnocultural Communities (20%), 6 through Regional Communities (13%), and 2 through the Temporary Public Policy (4%). The message is unmistakable: Manitoba wants to know why you specifically want to settle here, and who in Manitoba has invited you. A high EOI score without an invitation will not get you selected in 2026.

### Trend 2 - TRRP Removed Hospitality, Added 16 Trades (February 4, 2026)

The Temporary Resident Retention Pilot (TRRP) — the newer program that lets employers support their temporary resident employees for permanent residence — underwent its biggest change since launch on February 4, 2026. The entire Hospitality and Food Services sector was removed from eligibility, and 16 new skilled trades occupations were added. This affects thousands of temporary residents working in Manitoba's hospitality industry who had been planning to use TRRP as their path to PR. The pilot now focuses entirely on skilled trades, construction, manufacturing, and technical services. Hospitality workers affected by the change must reassess their pathway through the standard Skilled Worker in Manitoba stream — which requires 6 months of continuous full-time employment with a qualifying Manitoba employer.

### Trend 3 - Draw Sizes Shrinking, Selection More Targeted

In 2025, MPNP invited more than 7,700 candidates and regularly held draws of 100–400 LAAs. In 2026 the numbers have contracted: Draw #262 at 55, #263 at 47, #264 at 29, #265 at 72, #266 at 46, and #267 at 14. That reflects both nomination-allocation pressure and a deliberate strategy of precision over volume. The good news: Manitoba typically exhausts its full annual allocation — the quota usually gets issued, just through smaller, more employer-aligned draws rather than big open-pool selections.

## Every Manitoba MPNP Stream - 2026 Breakdown

MPNP has three main streams. The Skilled Worker Stream (two pathways) accounts for the vast majority of nominations. The International Education Stream serves Manitoba university and college graduates. The Business Investor Stream serves entrepreneurs.

### Skilled Worker Stream

#### Skilled Worker in Manitoba (SWM)

The SWM pathway is for temporary foreign workers and international student graduates already living and working in Manitoba. Your ongoing Manitoba employment is both your mandatory Manitoba connection AND your primary adaptability scoring factor — worth up to 500 EOI points.

Core eligibility requirements:

- Valid work permit and currently working full-time in Manitoba
- Minimum 6 months of continuous full-time employment with the Manitoba employer offering you the long-term job
- Long-term (permanent) full-time job offer from that employer
- Employer must be incorporated or registered under an act and operating an established business in Manitoba
- Minimum 60 points on the MPNP EOI human capital assessment
- Settlement Plan showing intention to live, work, and establish in Manitoba permanently
- Sufficient English or French language proficiency to perform the job duties

Important employer requirements:

- Employer must have an established production capacity, plant, or place of business in Manitoba
- Self-employed individuals, business owners, owner-operators, and independent contractors cannot apply under SWM — and experience gained in those capacities does not count as qualifying Manitoba employment
- MPNP issues work permit support letters only where a genuine employer-employee relationship exists

TRRP sub-pathway (within SWM): Manitoba's Temporary Resident Retention Pilot lets employers in specific sectors/occupations support their temporary resident employees for permanent residence. As of February 4, 2026: Hospitality and Food Services is NO LONGER ELIGIBLE, and 16 skilled trades occupations were added. TRRP requires 18 months of full-time employment with the same employer — longer than the standard SWM requirement of 6 months. See the dedicated TRRP section for the full 2026 update.

#### Skilled Worker Overseas (SWO)

The SWO pathway is for qualified skilled workers not currently working in Manitoba but able to demonstrate an established connection to the province. It also covers candidates already in Manitoba who do not yet meet the 6-month employment requirement for SWM.

A connection to Manitoba is an absolute prerequisite — no connection, no eligibility, regardless of EOI score. Three types of connection qualify:

Connection Type 1 - Manitoba Support (Friend or Relative): A Manitoba Supporter is an established Manitoba resident who is a friend or relative of the applicant, willing and able to endorse the applicant's settlement and employment plan. Close relatives may support more than one skilled worker simultaneously if they can demonstrate the ability to do so. Friends and distant relatives may support only one applicant at a time.

Connection Type 2 - Manitoba Experience (Previous Work or Study): You have previously worked or studied in Manitoba.

Connection Type 3 - Manitoba Invitation (Strategic Recruitment Initiative): You received a direct Invitation to Apply from the MPNP. This is the most powerful connection type in 2026 — because every draw this year has exclusively selected candidates holding this type of connection.

SWO eligibility (in addition to the Manitoba connection): minimum 60 MPNP points, skilled work experience, language, education, settlement funds, and demonstrated intention to settle permanently in Manitoba.

### International Education Stream (IES)

#### International Education Stream (IES)

The IES is Manitoba's dedicated pathway for graduates of Manitoba post-secondary institutions, and it carries one big advantage: no work experience requirement — making it potentially the fastest route to Manitoba PR for recent graduates. Key requirements: graduation from a Manitoba institution, a full-time program of sufficient length, a valid PGWP, a Manitoba job offer related to your field of study, CLB 7 minimum language, and intent to reside in Manitoba permanently.

2026 IES note: the IES targets Manitoba DLI graduates specifically. Unlike Saskatchewan SINP's Student Category (recently restricted), Manitoba's IES has not been similarly restricted — though it does require that you studied in Manitoba.

### Business Investor Stream (MPNP-B)

#### Business Investor Stream (MPNP-B)

The MPNP-B is for experienced business people planning to move to Manitoba and establish or purchase a business in the province. Note: Business Investor Stream EOI draws are NOT currently being conducted (2026). All EOIs received are reviewed within 4 weeks — MPNP issues either a Letter of Advice to Apply or a feedback letter. Contact us for a Business Investor Stream consultation — this is a highly specialized pathway that demands expert guidance.

## Building Your Manitoba Connection - The Most Critical Step

Every MPNP applicant under the Skilled Worker Stream must have at least one established connection to Manitoba. This is not a points category you can skip — it is an eligibility prerequisite. Without a connection, your EOI will not be accepted regardless of score. Here is every type of connection and exactly what it takes to qualify.

### Manitoba Employment (Most Powerful in 2026)

Up to 500 EOI adaptability points + SWM eligibility

This is the single most powerful Manitoba connection — and the dominant selection pathway in 2026. It requires a valid work permit, at least 6 months of continuous full-time employment with a Manitoba employer, and a long-term job offer from that same employer. Employer Services lets Manitoba employers directly nominate specific candidates through the MPNP. For clients without a Manitoba employer: our Hire Global Talent (HGT) employer division actively connects qualified immigration candidates with Manitoba employers in healthcare, technology, and skilled trades.

### Close Relative or Friend as Manitoba Supporter

Up to 200 EOI adaptability points + SWO eligibility

Having a close relative or established friend in Manitoba who is a Canadian citizen or permanent resident (living in Manitoba for at least 1 year) gives you the Manitoba Supporter connection. Eligible close relatives: sister, brother, aunt, uncle, niece, nephew, grandparent, or first cousin. Children cannot support parents for MPNP; spouses who are Canadian citizens or PRs do not qualify as Supporters.

### Previous Manitoba Work or Study Experience

Regional + adaptability points + SWO eligibility

If you have previously worked or studied in Manitoba, that experience establishes your connection. Documents proving prior Manitoba employment or study are required. This connection works best when combined with an active plan to return to Manitoba.

### Direct Strategic Recruitment Invitation from MPNP

Most valuable in 2026 - qualifies you for every 2026 draw

An official invitation from Manitoba — issued through Employer Services, Francophone Community, Ethnocultural Communities, Regional Communities, or Exploratory Visits — is the most critical connection type in 2026. For clients overseas with no Manitoba ties: VMC recommends beginning with the Francophone pathway (if you have French proficiency) or connecting with Manitoba employers through HGT.

## The MPNP EOI Ranking System - 1,000 Point Scale

The MPNP uses a detailed EOI ranking system that scores candidates out of 1,000 points across six factor categories. Points drive your ranking in the selection pool — but in 2026, where every draw has targeted strategic recruitment invitations, having a Manitoba invitation is a prerequisite for selection, not just a scoring advantage. Source: [immigratemanitoba.com/immigrate/apply/eoi/](https://immigratemanitoba.com/immigrate/apply/eoi/)

### Language Proficiency

Points based on CLB bands for reading, writing, listening, and speaking in your first official language. Accepted tests: IELTS, CELPIP, TEF Canada, TCF Canada. A second official language bonus at CLB 5+ requires valid test proof.

### Age at Time of EOI Submission

Points by age band. Peak points for the 21–45 age range.

### Skilled Work Experience

Points by total years of skilled work experience. More experience in higher-skilled NOC categories earns more points. Experience must be full-time and paid.

### Education and Training

Highest credential only. PhD maximum; master's strong; bachelor's high; 2-year diploma moderate. An ECA is required for foreign credentials.

### Adaptability - Manitoba Connections (Dominates EOI Scoring)

Manitoba Employment alone can be worth up to 500 of 1,000 points. You receive points only for your highest-scoring connection type — you cannot stack multiples. Regional immigration points may supplement.

### Risk Factor (Can Decrease Your Score)

Strong ties to other Canadian provinces (job offer, family, residence) deduct risk points — Manitoba prioritizes genuine settlement commitment.

### What Does It Actually Take to Get Selected in 2026?

The MPNP no longer selects based on EOI score alone. Every 2026 draw has required a strategic recruitment invitation. ## Temporary Resident Retention Pilot (TRRP) - Full 2026 Update

The TRRP is a pilot program within the Skilled Worker in Manitoba stream that lets Manitoba employers support their current temporary resident employees for permanent residence. It launched in September 2025 and was significantly restructured on February 4, 2026.

### Before Feb 4, 2026 - Eligible sectors included

Construction, Manufacturing, Transportation and Warehousing, Healthcare, Skilled Trades, and Accommodation and Food Services.

### After Feb 4, 2026

- Hospitality and Food Services removed — effective immediately.
- 16 skilled trades occupations added (occupation-based eligibility).
- Construction, Manufacturing, Transportation, Healthcare, and Skilled Trades remain eligible.

16 newly added skilled trades (NOC TEER 2-3)

TRRP eligibility (February 2026): valid work permit; 18 months continuous full-time employment with the same Manitoba employer; long-term job offer; employer in an eligible TRRP sector OR candidate in one of 16 exempt trades regardless of sector; minimum 60 MPNP EOI points; settlement funds ($10,000 principal + $2,000 per dependent); language per NOC TEER.

If a hospitality employer employs a worker in one of the 16 added occupations, that worker may still qualify through TRRP — the occupation exemption can override the sector exclusion.

## Complete MPNP Document Checklist

MPNP applications that are incomplete are returned. After receiving an LAA, you have 60 days to submit a complete application — so prepare documents well before any LAA is expected. All documents not in English or French must come with a certified translation from an IRCC-designated translator — not a family member, not your employer, and not anyone preparing your application.

- Valid passport — all pages. Must remain valid through to the issuance of your permanent residence.
- Birth certificate
- Marriage certificate (if married), Death certificate of former spouse (if widowed), Final divorce documents (for all prior divorces)
- For common-law partners: IMM5409 Statutory Declaration of Common-Law Union + 12-month cohabitation proof (joint bank account, joint lease, joint bills, joint insurance)
- Passports for all accompanying family members
- Birth certificates for all dependent children
- Custody and child support documents (if dependants are not accompanying)
