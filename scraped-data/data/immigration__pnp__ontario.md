## What Changed on June 26, 2026 - Ontario's Complete OINP Redesign

Ontario completed a two-step regulatory overhaul of the OINP. First, O. Reg 47/26 (announced March 16, 2026) amended O. Reg 421/17, repealing the framework for all nine OINP categories effective May 30, 2026. Then, O. Reg 422/17 came into force on June 26, 2026 — this is the operative date when the eight active OINP streams actually closed and the new Ontario Workforce Priority stream launched.

The eight streams that closed on June 26, 2026:

Employer Job Offer - Foreign Worker; Employer Job Offer - In-Demand Skills; Employer Job Offer - International Student; Master's Graduate; PhD Graduate; Express Entry Human Capital Priorities (HCP); Express Entry French-Speaking Skilled Worker; Express Entry Skilled Trades (which had been suspended since November 14, 2025 for systemic misrepresentation and fraud). A ninth category, Entrepreneur, had already been suspended since November 4, 2024.

The new Ontario Workforce Priority stream - three pathways:

The single replacement stream has three pathways. Pathway 1 - TEER 0-3: job offer required, CLB 6 in all four skills (CLB 5 for certain occupations — specific list pending from the province), post-secondary degree or diploma required. Pathway 2 - TEER 4-5: job offer required, CLB 4, Canadian secondary school diploma or equivalent, 9 months cumulative experience in the last 2 years in the job-offer position. Pathway 3 - Self-Employed Physicians: no job offer required; must be in good standing with the College of Physicians and Surgeons of Ontario (CPSO) and OHIP-eligible (NOC 31100-31102).

EOI portal status:

The EOI portal closed to new entries on June 26, 2026. Non-invited EOIs are being withdrawn. The new EOI system is expected to reopen later in summer 2026. The province has not provided a specific month or date. Phase 2 additions — including Exceptional Talent and Priority Healthcare streams — are expected later in 2026 but have not launched yet.

## Ontario's Provincial Nominee Program - The Basics

The Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program (OINP) is Ontario's provincial immigration program that allows the province to nominate skilled workers, international graduates, and entrepreneurs for Canadian permanent residence. With 14,119 nomination spaces in 2026 — a 31% increase from 10,750 in 2025 — Ontario holds the largest provincial allocation in Canada, representing approximately 15% of the national PNP total of 91,500.

How OINP leads to Canadian PR: Ontario uses OINP to identify workers and graduates who will contribute to the province's labour market. When Ontario nominates you, IRCC adds +600 CRS points to your Express Entry profile (for Express Entry-aligned streams) — in recent draws, well above every published cutoff (IRCC sets the cutoff each round). Federal PR processing after nomination takes approximately 6 months through Express Entry, or approximately 18 months through Base PNP (direct IRCC application outside Express Entry).

What 2026 means for OINP: Ontario drew aggressively in the first quarter of 2026 under its former streams — healthcare workers received 1,649 invitations in February 2026 alone, and physicians received 129 dedicated invitations. Then, on June 26, 2026, Ontario completed the largest overhaul in OINP history: eight legacy streams were closed and a single Ontario Workforce Priority stream launched in their place. The EOI portal is currently closed and expected to reopen later in summer 2026.

Ontario's 2026 immigration philosophy has shifted. The old OINP was a broad-net program — it ran general draws for any skilled worker in the Express Entry pool who met minimum criteria. The new OINP is a sniper rifle — targeted, sector-specific, occupation-driven. Healthcare workers, trades professionals, and workers with Ontario employer support have the highest probability of invitation in 2026 and beyond.

### 14,119 Spaces - 31% More Than 2025

Ontario received its largest ever nomination allocation in 2026 (up from 10,750 in 2025). The 2025 allocation was fully used by December 17, 2025. The 2026 allocation of 14,119 spaces supports the new Workforce Priority stream and any Phase 2 streams announced later in the year.

The Ontario Workforce Priority stream replaces all eight former OINP streams with three clear pathways: TEER 0-3 skilled workers (job offer + CLB 6 + post-secondary), TEER 4-5 essential workers (job offer + CLB 4 + 9 months experience), and self-employed physicians (CPSO registration + OHIP eligibility, no job offer). The old broad-net design is gone.

### Self-Employed Physicians - No Job Offer Required

The Self-Employed Physician pathway is part of the Ontario Workforce Priority stream launched June 26, 2026. This is the only pathway within the new stream that does NOT require a job offer. Physicians must be in good standing with the College of Physicians and Surgeons of Ontario (CPSO) and OHIP-eligible.

Eligible physician NOC codes:

- NOC 31100 - Specialists in clinical and laboratory medicine (cardiologists, dermatologists, psychiatrists, internists, etc.)
- NOC 31101 - Surgeons and surgical specialists (general surgeons, orthopaedic surgeons, neurosurgeons, etc.)
- NOC 31102 - General practitioners and family physicians

Who qualifies:

- CPSO registration in good standing (full or provisional certificate)
- OHIP-eligible
- Practising or intending to practise in Ontario
- Meet all standard OINP admissibility requirements

What you need instead of a job offer:

- CPSO registration certificate
- OHIP eligibility documentation
- Practice location or intended practice location in Ontario
- Evidence of medical practice (billing records, clinic letters)

Physician draws occurred under the former OINP streams before June 26, 2026 — the Workforce Priority stream continues to prioritize physician immigration through this no-job-offer pathway. The EOI portal is expected to reopen later in summer 2026.

## How to Apply for Ontario Workforce Priority - What to Do Now

The EOI portal is closed pending the new system. Here is the expected process for the Ontario Workforce Priority stream when the portal reopens, and what you should be doing now to prepare.

Process Flow A - TEER 0-3 / TEER 4-5 Pathways (Employer-Driven):

Ontario Workforce Priority - Employer Required

Step 1: "Secure an Ontario Job Offer in Your NOC"

Both the TEER 0-3 and TEER 4-5 pathways require a valid Ontario job offer. The job offer must be from an eligible Ontario employer in the correct NOC for your pathway. Your employer must be registered in the OINP employer portal. Confirm your TEER level before approaching employers so you know which pathway applies and what language and credential requirements you must meet.

Step 2: "Confirm Your Eligibility Pathway"

TEER 0-3 requires CLB 6 in all four skills and a post-secondary degree or diploma. TEER 4-5 requires CLB 4 and a Canadian secondary school diploma or equivalent, plus 9 months cumulative experience in the last 2 years in the same occupation as the job offer. Getting your TEER wrong means applying under the wrong pathway with the wrong requirements — book an assessment to confirm your correct pathway before investing in language testing or credential preparation.

Step 3: "Have Your Employer Register in the OINP Portal"

The OINP Employer Portal registration can take several days for employers doing it for the first time. Your employer needs to provide Ontario business registration, CRA business number, NAICS code, financial documents, and signing officer information. This should happen now — not after the EOI portal reopens. Employers who are already registered when the portal opens can submit job offers immediately.

Step 4: "Submit Your EOI When the Portal Reopens (Expected Summer 2026)"

When the Ontario Workforce Priority EOI portal reopens later in summer 2026, you and your employer submit your EOIs. Ontario will then run draws and issue invitations to candidates who meet the pathway requirements. A strict application window (similar to the 17-day deadline under former streams) is expected to apply — specific deadlines will be confirmed when the portal reopens. The OINP application fee is CAD $1,500 (non-refundable).

Step 5: "OINP Reviews Your Application"

OINP processing timelines for the Workforce Priority stream will be confirmed when the portal opens. Under former streams, processing typically took 90-180 days. OINP may request additional documents during review.

Step 6: "Receive Provincial Nomination and Federal PR"

If approved, you receive a Nomination Letter. For Express Entry-aligned pathways, IRCC adds +600 CRS points to your profile — in recent draws, well above every published cutoff (IRCC sets the cutoff each round). After the ITA, you have 60 days to submit your federal PR application to IRCC. Federal processing takes approximately 6 months. VMC assists with both the OINP application and the federal PR application.

Process Flow B - Self-Employed Physicians:

Self-Employed Physician Pathway - No Job Offer Required

Step 1: "Confirm CPSO Good Standing Registration"

Your CPSO registration must be in good standing — full certificate or provisional certificate. If you are an internationally trained physician pursuing CPSO registration, confirm your registration status and whether your certificate is acceptable for the OINP Workforce Priority pathway. VMC advises on CPSO registration documentation requirements.

Step 2: "Confirm OHIP Eligibility"

You must be OHIP-eligible. This means you are eligible to bill OHIP for medical services in Ontario. Gather your OHIP eligibility documentation and any correspondence confirming your eligibility status.

Step 3: "Prepare Your OINP Application Package"

Unlike the employer-required pathways, you do not need a job offer. Instead, gather:

- CPSO registration certificate (full or provisional)
- OHIP eligibility documentation
- Evidence of Ontario practice location or intended practice location
- Evidence of medical practice (billing records, clinic letters)
- NOC must be 31100, 31101, or 31102

Step 4: "Submit When EOI Portal Reopens (Expected Summer 2026)"

When the Ontario Workforce Priority EOI portal reopens later in summer 2026, physicians submit their EOI. The application fee is CAD $1,500 (non-refundable). OINP reviews the application and, if approved, issues a Nomination Letter. If the pathway is Express Entry-aligned, IRCC adds +600 CRS points and a federal ITA follows at the next draw. Federal PR processing takes approximately 6 months.

## The Most Common OINP Mistakes - and How We Prevent Them

These mistakes cause refusals, missed deadlines, and wasted application fees. We have seen every one of them.

### Not Confirming Your TEER Level Before the Portal Opens

Under the Workforce Priority stream, your TEER level determines which pathway you use. TEER 0-3 requires CLB 6 and post-secondary credentials; TEER 4-5 requires CLB 4 and 9 months experience. Getting your TEER wrong means using the wrong pathway, wrong language requirements, and a rejected application. VMC confirms your correct TEER level and which pathway applies before you invest in language testing or document preparation.

### Missing the 17-Day or 45-Day Deadline

OINP deadlines are absolute. Miss them by one day and your invitation is cancelled — permanently. You cannot request an extension. You cannot resubmit the same invitation. You must wait for the next draw and hope to be invited again. VMC prepares complete application packages BEFORE you receive an ITA or NOI, so the moment the invitation arrives, your team is ready to submit within days — not scrambling.

### Inadequate Language for Your TEER Level

TEER 0-3 requires CLB 6 (or CLB 5 for certain occupations — list pending from province). TEER 4-5 requires CLB 4. Getting this wrong means applying under the wrong pathway or missing the language threshold entirely. VMC assesses your language test results against the correct Workforce Priority threshold for your NOC and advises whether a new test is needed before the portal opens.

### Employer Reference Letters That Don't Meet Standards

OINP has specific requirements for employment reference letters. They must be on company letterhead, include the company's CRA business number, list your specific job duties (not generic descriptions), state your salary and hours, and be signed by a supervisor — not a family member or business partner. Many candidates receive letters that look professional but fail OINP's documentary standards. We provide employers with a template that meets every OINP requirement.

### Employer Not Prepared for Their 14-Day Deadline

In the Employer Job Offer streams, your employer has 14 days to submit their side of the application. Many candidates receive an ITA and immediately contact their employer — only to find out the employer doesn't know what the OINP portal is, hasn't registered their business, and needs weeks to gather their financial documents. By the time the employer is ready, the 14-day window has closed and the invitation is cancelled. We work with your employer BEFORE any ITA arrives to ensure they are fully registered and document-ready.

### Waiting Without Preparing

The EOI portal is closed, but that does not mean there is nothing to do. Candidates who use this window to secure an Ontario job offer, confirm their employer's OINP portal registration, get their language test results ready, and complete a full OINP eligibility assessment will be first in line when the portal reopens. Waiting passively means scrambling when the portal opens — and potentially missing the first draw window. VMC runs Workforce Priority assessments now so clients are ready on day one.
