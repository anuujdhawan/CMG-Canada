## What is a PR Card and Why It Matters

The Permanent Resident (PR) Card is the official proof of your status as a permanent resident of Canada. It matters most at the border: to board a plane, bus, train, or boat back into Canada, you must show a valid PR card. It is valid for 5 years for most adults and 1 year for some children under 14. Keeping the card current — and meeting the residency obligation behind it — protects your status and your freedom to travel.

## PR Card Validity

Most PR cards are issued with a 5-year validity for adults. Children under 14 may receive a card valid for 1 year. The expiry date is printed on the front of the card, and IRCC expects you to plan ahead: renew before expiry, especially if travel is on your calendar. You cannot use an expired card to re-enter Canada.

## Residency Obligation: 730 Days in 5 Years

Here is the rule that surprises most people: to keep PR status, you must be physically present in Canada for at least 730 days — two full years — within every rolling 5-year period. Not all time abroad counts against you. Days spent outside Canada with a Canadian citizen spouse or common-law partner, or while working for a Canadian business or the public service, may be credited. Fail the obligation and you risk losing PR status when you renew your card or re-enter the country.

## How to Calculate Residency

Counting is straightforward once you know the window: tally every day you were physically in Canada during the 5 years before the day you sign your application. Border officers use the same math when you arrive. Keep a careful travel history — entry and exit stamps, boarding passes, and your own records — because vague memory will not satisfy an officer. IRCC's online residency calculator is a good starting point, and we run the calculation for every client before they sign anything.

## Renewal Process

- Confirm you meet the residency obligation before applying.
- Complete the PR card renewal application and assemble your documents: current card, government-issued ID, photos, and the processing fee.
- Submit online through your IRCC account or by mail, following the current instructions.
- Wait for processing; you may be asked to attend an in-person card pick-up appointment.

## Lost, Stolen, or Damaged PR Card

Losing your PR card is stressful, but replaceable. Use the same renewal application form to apply for a replacement, report the loss if the card was stolen, provide identification, and pay the fee. Processing times apply, so if you have travel planned, flag it early — we have helped clients rush replacements ahead of booked flights.

## PR Travel Document (PRTD)

If you are outside Canada and your PR card has expired, was lost, or never arrived, you cannot board a flight home without a Permanent Resident Travel Document (PRTD). You apply for a PRTD at a Canadian visa office abroad. The catch: you must still meet the residency obligation to receive one. For permanent residents stranded abroad, the PRTD is the difference between a quick return and an extended stay away.

## Processing Time 2025

PR card and PRTD processing times move throughout the year, so always check IRCC's current published times before making travel plans. The official processing times page is the reference our consultants use when advising clients on whether to apply for renewal or wait.

## Failed Residency Obligation

Not meeting the 730-day requirement can lead to a refused renewal or PRTD — and in serious cases, loss of PR status. There are limited remedies, including humanitarian and compassionate (H&C) considerations that let an officer weigh your circumstances, your ties to Canada, and the impact on your family. These cases are nuanced and fact-specific; we review the full picture and advise on the strongest route before you file anything.
