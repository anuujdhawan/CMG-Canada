## What Constitutes Misrepresentation?

Misrepresentation means providing false or misleading information - or withholding material facts - in an application, at an interview, or at the border. The key word is "material": the information must be capable of affecting the decision. Here is the uncomfortable truth about Canadian immigration law: even unintentional errors can be found to be misrepresentation if they are material. Honesty and full disclosure are not just virtues here; they are your best defence. IRCC's own guidance on [misrepresentation](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/application-forms-guides/guide-5521-applying-change-conditions-extend-your-stay-canada.html) is a useful baseline, but the law itself (section 40 of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act) is what governs.

## Consequences

A finding of misrepresentation can trigger a refusal of your application, a 5-year ban from Canada, and a permanent record that follows you into every future application. In serious cases, existing status or even permanent residence can be revoked. These consequences are severe, which is why a thorough, well-timed response to any Procedural Fairness Letter is not optional - it is essential.

## Procedural Fairness Letter - How to Respond

If IRCC sends you a PFL about misrepresentation, you usually have 15-30 days to respond. Your response must do three things: (1) address every single concern raised in the letter, (2) provide a clear explanation backed by supporting evidence, and (3) correct any genuine errors while demonstrating that you did not intend to mislead, where that is the truth. Miss the deadline and IRCC may make a finding of misrepresentation without your input - automatically. We help clients draft strong, evidence-based responses and, most importantly, meet the deadline.

## Common Scenarios

- Third-party consultant errors - You relied on a representative who made mistakes. You can explain this and show that you provided accurate information to the representative; the error was theirs, not a deliberate act of yours.
- Translation mistakes - A document was mistranslated. Provide a corrected translation with an explanation of what went wrong.
- Omitted information - You failed to disclose something material. Explain why and give the complete picture; H&C submissions may be relevant to your situation.
- Fraudulent documents (agent fraud) - You were unaware a document was fraudulent. You must demonstrate you did not know and took reasonable steps to verify it.

## Our team's Approach

We treat misrepresentation and PFL responses as urgent matters, because they are. We analyze the letter line by line, identify every concern, gather evidence (including affidavits, corrected documents, and legal opinions where they help), and draft a targeted response before the deadline.
