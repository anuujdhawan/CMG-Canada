## Page Content

## What is Judicial Review?

Judicial Review is the process by which the Federal Court examines an immigration decision - a refusal, a misrepresentation finding, or a removal order - to determine whether it was made fairly and in accordance with the law. The court does not substitute its own decision for IRCC's; it either upholds the decision or sends the matter back for a fresh decision. Only lawyers (or you, self-represented) can argue before the Federal Court - RCICs cannot appear in court - which is why we work alongside authorized immigration lawyers who can.

## When to Apply (Deadlines)

For most immigration decisions, you must apply for leave (permission to proceed) within 30 days of the decision. Refugee matters often carry a tighter 15-day deadline. These deadlines are strict, and missing one generally closes the door to Judicial Review entirely. The moment you receive a negative decision, contact us or a lawyer - do not wait for the weekend to end.

## Leave Application Process

Judicial Review begins with an application for "leave." If leave is granted, the court will hear your full application. The process involves filing documents, potentially a hearing, and a decision. If the court quashes the decision, IRCC (or the tribunal) will typically have to re-decide your case - often with fresh eyes and sometimes a different officer.

## Grounds for Judicial Review

Common grounds include: procedural fairness (you were not given a fair opportunity to respond), errors of law (the decision-maker misapplied the law), and unreasonable outcomes (the decision was not justified by the evidence). A lawyer will identify which grounds are strongest for your specific case - that assessment is worth doing early, before the clock runs out.

## Stay of Removal - Urgent Relief

If you are facing removal from Canada, you may need a stay of removal so that you are not removed while the court considers your Judicial Review. Stays are extremely time-sensitive and demand strong grounds. We can refer you to a lawyer who handles urgent stay applications, often within days.

## Mandamus - Force IRCC to Decide

A Mandamus application asks the court to order IRCC (or another decision-maker) to actually make a decision when there has been unreasonable delay. It does not ask the court to decide the outcome of your application - only to compel a decision to be made. Mandamus becomes relevant when processing has dragged far beyond published times with no reasonable explanation. IRCC's [processing times tool](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/check-processing-times.html) is the reference point for what "unreasonable" means.

## Our team's Role

RCICs cannot represent clients in Federal Court - that is the law. What we can do is assess your case, identify whether Judicial Review is worth pursuing, prepare the factual and documentary foundation, and refer you to authorized immigration lawyers who will file the leave application, stay motion, or mandamus application. We stay involved to keep your file coherent between the consultant side and the legal side.

## Cost and Timeline

Judicial Review involves court fees plus legal fees. Timelines vary widely: a leave application can take months, and a full review adds more time on top of that. Your lawyer will give you a realistic cost and timeline estimate before you commit.

## Success Rates - Honest Assessment

Success depends entirely on the strength of your grounds - procedural fairness, error of law, or unreasonableness. Not every refusal can be successfully challenged, and we will not pretend otherwise. A lawyer can give you an honest assessment of whether Judicial Review is worth the investment in your case.
