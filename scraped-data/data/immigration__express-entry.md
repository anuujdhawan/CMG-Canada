## Three Pathways Into Express Entry

### International work experience

- 1+ yr foreign skilled work (TEER 0-3)
- CLB 7 language score
- Min. 67 pts on FSW grid
- ECA for foreign education

### Already working in Canada

- 1+ yr Canadian skilled work
- NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3
- CLB 7 (TEER 0/1) or CLB 5 (TEER 2/3)
- Most active stream 2025-2026

### Tradesperson seeking PR

- 2+ yrs trades experience
- Job offer OR certificate of qualification
- CLB 5 reading/writing, CLB 4 speaking/listening
- Eligible trade category
2026 Update: No General Draws Since April 2024

Here's something most people don't realise yet: IRCC has run only category-based and CEC draws since May 2024. If your CRS sits below 520, your fastest realistic path is category eligibility (French, Healthcare, Trades) or a PNP nomination worth +600 points. Job offer CRS points were also removed in March 2025. Start with a free assessment and we'll tell you honestly which road is fastest for you.

### Federal Skilled Worker (FSW)

For skilled workers with international work experience

- Minimum 1 year continuous skilled work experience (NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3) in the last 10 years
- CLB 7 in all four language abilities (IELTS Academic/General or CELPIP)
- Minimum 67 points on the FSW selection grid (education, language, experience, age, adaptability)
- Valid ECA (Educational Credential Assessment) for foreign degrees
- Proof of funds unless you have a valid Canadian job offer or are currently working in Canada
- Job offers: no longer add CRS points as of March 25, 2025 (IRCC policy change)
CRS Strategy for FSW applicants:

Because no general draws have run since April 2024, FSW applicants need a sharper strategy: push language to CLB 9+ (worth 20–40 extra points), pick up French (French category draws have landed at CRS 393–419), secure a PNP nomination (+600 points), or build Canadian experience to switch into CEC. You can verify the official FSW requirements directly on IRCC's Federal Skilled Worker page.

### Canadian Experience Class (CEC)

For temporary workers building their future in Canada

- Minimum 1 year Canadian skilled work experience (NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3) in the last 3 years
- CLB 7 for NOC TEER 0 or 1 occupations
- CLB 5 for NOC TEER 2 or 3 occupations
- Plan to live outside Quebec (Quebec has its own immigration system)
- No education requirement (but education adds CRS points)
CRS Reality - CEC Draws 2026: CRS 507-515

CEC has been the workhorse of Express Entry since 2024, with frequent dedicated draws. If your score is under 507, the levers that matter are French (opens the 393–419 French draws), a provincial nomination, or another language attempt.

International Students: PGWP → CEC → PR

Graduated in Canada with a PGWP? Twelve months of full-time TEER 0-3 work qualifies you for CEC. One 2024 rule to remember: PGWP now requires graduating in an eligible field (healthcare, STEM, trades, agriculture, or education). IRCC's Canadian Experience Class page is the authoritative place to confirm current eligibility.

### Federal Skilled Trades (FST)

For tradespeople with in-demand skills

- 2 years full-time skilled trades experience in the last 5 years
- CLB 5 reading and writing; CLB 4 listening and speaking
- Valid job offer of at least 2 years OR certificate of qualification from a Canadian province or territory
- Eligible trade categories: industrial/electrical/construction, maintenance & equipment, supervisors in natural resources, processing & manufacturing
Trades Category Draws:

Trade category draws have ranged from CRS 388 (2023) to 477 (April 2026), and many tradespeople qualify under both FST and the Trades category draw. IRCC's Federal Skilled Trades eligibility page shows the full official list.

- What is Express Entry and is it the right pathway for me? Express Entry is Canada's primary system for selecting skilled immigrants for permanent residence. It manages three federal programs: Federal Skilled Worker (FSW), Canadian Experience Class (CEC), and Federal Skilled Trades (FST). You create an online profile, receive a Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score, and wait for IRCC to issue an Invitation to Apply (ITA) in periodic draws. In 2026, IRCC has been holding draws roughly every week - issuing over 49,000 ITAs in just the first 11 weeks of the year. It is the fastest route to Canadian PR for most skilled workers. Whether it is right for you depends on your education, language scores, work experience, and whether you have Canadian connections. We offer a free assessment to identify your strongest pathway. IRCC's official Express Entry page is the best starting point for the formal rules.
- What is the difference between FSW, CEC, and FST under Express Entry? Federal Skilled Worker (FSW) is for people outside Canada with at least 1 year of foreign skilled work experience in NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3, a language score of CLB 7 or higher in all four abilities, and completed post-secondary education. It uses a 67-point selection grid before CRS scoring applies. Canadian Experience Class (CEC) is for people who already have at least 1 year of skilled work experience inside Canada in the past 3 years - no minimum education required. In 2026, CEC has been the most active draw category with CRS cutoffs around 507. Federal Skilled Trades (FST) is for tradespeople with 2 years of experience and either a valid job offer or a certificate of qualification in their trade. CEC candidates typically receive ITAs faster due to higher CRS scores from Canadian experience.
- What is a CRS score and how is it calculated? The Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) is a points-based system used to rank Express Entry candidates. Your score is based on: age (up to 110 points if applying alone, 100 with a spouse), education (up to 150 points alone), language skills in English and French (up to 310 points alone - the single largest factor), and Canadian work experience (up to 80 points). Additional points come from a spouse's education and language scores, having a sibling in Canada (15 points), Canadian education, French language ability, and provincial nomination (600 points - placing candidates well above recent draw cutoffs). The maximum score is 1,200. In 2026, most general pool candidates score between 480 and 520. Category draws have seen cutoffs as low as 169 for physicians and 397 for French-language candidates.
- What is an Invitation to Apply (ITA) and what happens after I get one? An ITA is a formal invitation from IRCC to submit a complete permanent residence application. Once you receive an ITA, you have exactly 60 days to submit - missing this deadline means starting over from scratch. During those 60 days you must gather: a completed medical exam from an IRCC-designated physician, police certificates from every country you lived in for 6 or more months since age 18, reference letters from all employers, your ECA if education was completed abroad, language test results, passport copies, and all family member documents. The government fee is CAD $1,365 per adult applicant. IRCC targets a 6-month processing time for complete applications. We begin document preparation with clients before an ITA arrives so the 60-day window is never wasted.
- What is a category-based draw and how does it affect my chances in 2026? Category-based draws allow IRCC to invite candidates with specific skills or language abilities at lower CRS scores than general draws. In 2026, this has transformed Express Entry. Active 2026 categories include: Canadian Experience Class, French language proficiency (cutoffs as low as 393-397), healthcare and social services, STEM (cybersecurity specialists), trades, transport, physicians with Canadian experience (record low CRS of 169 in February 2026), senior managers with Canadian experience (CRS 429 in March 2026), researchers, and national defence. Agriculture and agri-food was removed in 2026. If your occupation falls in a priority category, you may receive an ITA at a CRS score 50 to 300 points below the general pool cutoff. We monitor every draw and alerts clients within hours when a category draw matches their profile. IRCC's category-based selection page outlines how the categories are set each year.
